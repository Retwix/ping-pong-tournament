## Framing

All four table corners sit well inside the frame, so the homography is
buildable. Two deviations from §2 that will shape results:

- **Low and oblique**, roughly standing height at a corner, not 2–2.5 m down the
  long axis. §2 notes side-on gives *cleaner* bounce detection — a bounce is a
  clean vertical minimum — at the cost of the near player occluding the table.
  Expect occlusion of the near half.
- **No margin beyond the ends.** A ball going long leaves frame almost at once,
  so floor bounces will rarely be seen. §7 calls those the strongest rally-end
  signal; rally ends here will mostly come from the dwell timeout instead, which
  means later and less certain.

No specular glare on the table. The surface is matte.

## Ground truth

`rally.truth.csv` holds 12 marked points, 5 left / 7 right, from 59.1 s to
175.1 s, gaps of 6–17 s.

**One rally in the clip is not a point.** A ball was played early on to decide
who serves. It has net crossings, table bounces and a rally end, so it will pass
every guard in §8 and the service will emit a point for it — correctly. It is
not in the csv, so a naive comparison scores it as a phantom point and penalises
the system for working.

Its exact time is not yet pinned down. Whole-frame motion is dominated by people
and the dog and does not isolate it. It will be unambiguous once the tracker
exists: a detected rally with no matching mark, in a clip where every other
rally has one. Annotate it then as `none` (the `n` key now records this live).

Everything before the first mark at 59.1 s is non-scoring.

## The orange gate, tuned

`DEFAULT_GATE`'s original `sat_min` of 110 was a guess, and it was far too low.
At 110 the gate matched a blob in 100% of frames at a median 2037 px²: human
skin. An arm is an 11,498 px² blob. That briefly looked like "colour cannot be
the primary discriminator", which was wrong — it was an untuned threshold, not a
failure of colour.

Swept on a frame with the ball visible near the camera:

| sat_min | blobs ≥15 px² | the ball | next largest |
|---|---|---|---|
| 100 | 44 | 1561 | **11498** (arm) |
| 140 | 36 | 1154 | 480 |
| 180 | 17 | **916** | 132 |
| 220 | **2** | **348** | 16 |

Skin collapses by 140. The ball survives past 240, shrinking gracefully as it
loses its dim edge pixels — the signature of a genuinely saturated object rather
than a marginal one.

**`sat_min = 180` is the setting**, now the default. Sampled through
`ball-positions.mp4` it yields *usually exactly one blob in the whole frame*,
and it is the ball — found at 10 of 11 sampled moments:

| ball at | area |
|---|---|
| far end | ~100–170 px² |
| mid table | ~180–430 px² |
| near camera | ~900 px² |

That ~9× spread between near and far is the depth-aware size gate from §5,
measured rather than guessed.

**What is still open.** All of the above is a ball at rest or moving slowly.
During a rally at 30 fps a struck ball smears across several pixels, and motion
blur mixes it with the background — lowering its saturation exactly when it
matters. Whether 180 holds during flight is M2's first measurement, and the
honest answer is that nothing here proves it does.

## Other things in the room

Also in frame: a **TV showing the live scorer**, with a large saturated red
panel. Outside the table polygon and far too large for the size gate, so it
should be rejected twice over — but it is a moving, saturated region and worth
knowing about before it surprises someone.
